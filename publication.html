Real-World Adaptation and Enhancement of Recurrent Marginal Structural Networks to Estimate Causal Impact of Sales and Account Management Teams

Zhiyuan Shen, Shuo Yang, et al. (including Emmanuel Ekwedike)
Show all 13 authorsZhiyuan Shen, Shuo Yang, Ashish Patira, Hossein Keshavarz, Michelle Song, Saad Zaheer, Palash Siddamsettiwar, Joey Kao, Nicole Cheng, Emmanuel Ekwedike, Matthew Sisco, Lei Kang, and Khalifeh AlJadda

In plain termsSales teams and AI assistants reach out to advertisers again and again over time, which makes it hard to tell which outreach actually moved the needle. This work adapts a deep-learning method for cause-and-effect analysis so Google can estimate the real, added impact of each sales program.

Why it mattersLeaders can invest in the human and AI programs that genuinely drive results, instead of the ones that only appear to.

  • Applied AI
  • Causal Inference
  • Machine Learning
  • Business Impact
  • Decision-Making
Read the paper OpenReview
Technical summary

In industrial settings like Google Customer Solutions (GCS), estimating the incremental value of human and agentic-AI sales interventions is challenging because treatments are dynamic and sequential, and their assignment is influenced by complex time-varying confounders that also affect advertiser outcomes. To address these challenges, we introduce several methodological extensions to the Recurrent Marginal Structural Networks technique. The resulting framework supports scalable estimation and attribution of longitudinal treatment effects across heterogeneous sales programs.

arXiv preprint2024

Solving Trojan Detection Competitions with Linear Weight Classification

Todd Huster, Peter Lin, Razvan Stefanescu, Emmanuel Ekwedike, and Ritu Chadha

In plain termsAn AI model can be secretly altered so it behaves normally until a hidden trigger appears. This method spots altered models by reading patterns in their internal weights, and it performed well across several detection competitions and application areas.

Why it mattersOrganizations adopting AI models they didn't build need a way to check those models can be trusted.

  • AI Security
  • Responsible AI
  • Machine Learning
Read the paper arXiv
Technical summary

Neural networks can conceal malicious Trojan backdoors that allow a trigger to covertly change the model behavior. Detecting signs of these backdoors, particularly without access to any triggered data, is the subject of ongoing research and open challenges. In one common formulation of the problem, we are given a set of clean and poisoned models and need to predict whether a given test model is clean or poisoned. In this paper, we introduce a detector that works remarkably well across many of the existing datasets and domains. It is obtained by training a binary classifier on a large number of models' weights after performing a few different pre-processing steps including feature selection and standardization, reference model weights subtraction, and model alignment prior to detection. We evaluate this algorithm on a diverse set of Trojan detection benchmarks and domains and examine the cases where the approach is most and least effective.

Queueing Systems · Springer2023

Group Symmetries and Bike Sharing for M/M/1/k Queueing Transience

Emmanuel Ekwedike, Jamol J. Pender, Robert C. Hampshire, and William A. Massey

In plain termsBike-share stations fill up and run empty in ways that are mathematically hard to predict. This paper derives exact formulas for how a station's bike count changes over time, using symmetry and complex analysis.

Why it mattersBetter predictions lead to better decisions about moving bikes around a city-wide system.

  • Operations Research
  • Optimization
  • Decision-Making
Read the paper Springer
Technical summary

Fundamental stochastic models for studying the dynamics of bike-sharing systems can be found within the transient behavior of the M/M/1/k queue and related stopped processes. We develop new techniques involving group symmetries and complex analysis to obtain exact solutions for their transition probabilities. These methods are based on the underlying Markovian structure of these random processes.

arXiv preprint2021

TOP: Backdoor Detection in Neural Networks via Transferability of Perturbation

Todd Huster and Emmanuel Ekwedike

In plain termsA tampered ("backdoored") AI model has a telltale sign: a tiny change that fools it on one image tends to fool it on other images too. Checking for that sign reveals tampering without needing the original training data.

Why it mattersA practical audit for AI models you didn't train yourself.

  • AI Security
  • Responsible AI
  • Machine Learning
Read the paper arXiv
Technical summary

Deep neural networks (DNNs) are vulnerable to “backdoor” poisoning attacks, in which an adversary implants a secret trigger into an otherwise normally functioning model. Detection of backdoors in trained models without access to the training data or example triggers is an important open problem. In this paper, we identify an interesting property of these models: adversarial perturbations transfer from image to image more readily in poisoned models than in clean models.

ICML2018

Feedback-Based Tree Search for Reinforcement Learning

Daniel R. Jiang, Emmanuel Ekwedike, and Han Liu

In plain termsCombines tree search, the planning technique behind game-playing AI, with deep neural networks that learn by repeatedly planning over short stretches of time. The resulting agent competed effectively in a popular multiplayer online battle arena game.

Why it mattersLearning to plan a sequence of decisions is the same skill AI needs in logistics, operations and strategy.

  • Reinforcement Learning
  • Decision-Making
  • Optimization
  • Machine Learning
Read the paper ICML
Technical summary

We describe a technique that iteratively applies MCTS on batches of small, finite-horizon versions of the original infinite-horizon MDP. We show that a deep neural network implementation of the technique can create a competitive AI agent for a popular multi-player online battle arena (MOBA) game.

IEEE CNS2015

Highly Scalable Verifiable Encrypted Search

Whitney A. Drazen, Emmanuel Ekwedike, and Rosario Gennaro

In plain termsLets a server search a database of encrypted documents without ever seeing the data or the keys, while the person searching can verify the results are complete and correct, even at large scale.

Why it mattersPrivate, verifiable search is a building block for cloud services people can trust with sensitive data.

  • Data Privacy
Read the paper IEEE Xplore
Technical summary

In encrypted search, a server holds an encrypted database of documents but not the keys under which the documents are encrypted. The server answers keyword queries from a client with the list of documents matching the query. In this paper we present two highly scalable protocols to search over encrypted data which achieve full security against a possibly malicious server and support conjunctive queries, where the client submits many keywords and asks the server to identify the documents that match all the keywords.
